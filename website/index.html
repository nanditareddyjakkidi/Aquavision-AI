01 / THE SUNLIT WORLD

Life thrives
in the light.

Sunlight gives shallow reefs their extraordinary detail. Fish move through coral habitats while currents carry plankton and suspended sediment.

Even here, clear water does not guarantee a clear photograph. The distance between a camera and its subject changes how much light reaches the lens.

0–200 mThe approximate sunlight zone.

02 / WHEN THE VIEW CHANGES

Water changes
the way we see.

Depth is only part of the story. Light, particles and movement all shape an underwater image.

01

Colors disappear

Water absorbs wavelengths differently. Red light weakens rapidly, often leaving a blue or green color cast.

02

Particles scatter light

Sediment and plankton redirect light. This can produce haze, low contrast and bright backscatter near a lamp.

03

Details soften

Low light, camera motion and forward scattering can obscure edges. Greater camera-to-object distance makes recognition harder.

03 / BEYOND THE SUNLIGHT

Deeper water.
Less light.

Between approximately 200 and 1,000 meters, sunlight fades through the twilight zone. Below it, imaging generally needs artificial illumination.

A bright shallow reef and a dark deep-sea scene present very different challenges. One correction cannot recover every detail that the camera never recorded.

04 / WHAT DOESN'T BELONG

A living ocean.
A human footprint.

Debris can settle among reefs, drift through the water or become caught on marine life. Its appearance changes underwater.

01

Bags & packaging

Transparent, folded material can blend into the seabed and obscure its own shape.

02

Bottles & containers

Sediment, damage and biological growth can hide familiar outlines.

03

Ropes & fishing gear

Thin strands and tangled nets can be difficult to distinguish from natural textures.
